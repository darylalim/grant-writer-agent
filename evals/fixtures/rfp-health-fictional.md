# Rural Communicable Disease Prevention and Surveillance Program

**Notice of Funding Opportunity, Fiscal Year 2027**
**Funding opportunity number:** RCDPS-27-001 (fictional)
**Assistance listing:** fictional
**Instrument:** Cooperative agreement

## 1. Program purpose

This program supports rural local health departments, health districts, and
community health centers to reduce preventable illness from communicable
disease in counties with fewer than 50,000 residents. Rural residents are more
likely to be diagnosed late with HIV and hepatitis C, less likely to have been
tested at all, and travel farther for treatment. Rural health departments also
detect community outbreaks later than urban ones, because clinical testing is
sparse and case reports arrive days after illness begins.

Applicants propose an integrated project with three components:

1. **Component A, community outreach.** Community health workers (CHWs) who
   live in the service area reach residents who do not use clinical care,
   with health education, vaccination navigation, and referral.
2. **Component B, testing and linkage to care.** Routine opt-out and
   community-based testing for HIV, hepatitis C, and syphilis, with linkage to
   treatment and to HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP), and integration with
   existing syringe services programs where state law permits them.
3. **Component C, community-level surveillance.** Wastewater-based
   surveillance at the service area's municipal treatment plants, analysed by
   the state public health laboratory, so that rising community levels of
   respiratory and enteric pathogens are seen before clinical cases are
   reported.

## 2. Eligibility

Eligible applicants are local or tribal health departments, multi-county
health districts, and federally qualified health centers whose service area is
at least 75% rural by the Federal Office of Rural Health Policy definition.
Applicants proposing Component C must include a letter of commitment from the
state public health laboratory and from each participating treatment plant.

### 4.3 Approach: Component B

Describe the testing model (clinic-based opt-out, community-based, or both),
the settings and populations prioritised, how a reactive result is confirmed,
how a person diagnosed is linked to treatment within 30 days, how PrEP is
offered to people who test negative and are at elevated risk, and how the
project works with syringe services programs. Describe how confidentiality is
protected in small communities where staff and clients know each other.

### 4.4 Approach: Component C

Describe the treatment plants and the population each serves, the sampling
schedule and method (24-hour composite influent samples are preferred), the
pathogen targets, how samples reach the state public health laboratory and in
what time, and how results are interpreted, reported to the health officer,
and communicated to the public. Targets must include SARS-CoV-2 and influenza
A and B, and should include at least one of: respiratory syncytial virus,
measles, mpox, norovirus, or influenza A(H5). Describe what level or trend
triggers a public health action, and what that action is. Analysis is
performed by the laboratory; applicants do not perform laboratory work.

### 4.5 Evaluation and Performance Measurement Plan

Describe the process and outcome measures for each component, the data
sources and how often each is collected, who conducts the evaluation, and how
findings are used to improve the program during the period of performance.
Awardees report the program's core measures semi-annually:

- number of people tested for HIV, hepatitis C, and syphilis, by setting;
- percentage of people newly diagnosed who are linked to care within 30 days;
- number of people prescribed PrEP;
- number of CHW contacts, and referrals completed;
- for Component C, the percentage of scheduled samples analysed and reported
  within 7 days of collection.

| Criterion | Points |

**Evaluation and performance measurement (20 points).** The extent to which
the plan measures each component's process and outcomes, uses data sources
the applicant has or can obtain, names who is responsible, collects the
required core measures, and uses findings during the project rather than only
at its end.

**Approach (40 points).** The extent to which each component is feasible in
the service area, is based on evidence, reaches the populations least served
now, protects confidentiality, and, for Component C, states the action levels
and the action each triggers.
